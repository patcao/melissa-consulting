Hi! I’m Melissa.

I know the college application process can be a stressful time for many students, and I’m here to help. As an MIT alum and former admissions officer, I’ve read more than 6000 domestic and international applications and participated in admissions committee discussions. I also worked closely with the 4000+ global alumni interviewer network at MIT, giving me a firsthand look at how essays, extracurriculars, the interview, academics, and personal context come together to shape an admissions reader's understanding of a student.

Today, I work with MIT undergraduates as an academic advisor and volunteer with Minds Matter Boston, where I mentor high-achieving students from low-income backgrounds through the college process. These experiences have shown me all sides of the application process, from the early high school years to the senior year application madness and even to college life after the application.

Many students find the college essay intimidating because they think they have to write what the admissions committee wants to hear, but what they are really looking for is who you are as a person and what you will contribute to campus. As an essay coach, my goal is to help you figure out what that story is and how best to express it in conjunction with the other parts of your application, but also to make sure that each student leaves feeling like they’ve gained more clarity on who they are and where they’re going.

WHAT WORKING TOGETHER LOOKS LIKE

I work one-on-one with students to help them turn their experiences into a compelling story for college applications. My approach is student-centered, focused on getting to know the student personally so that I can help advise on how to connect their specific background and interests to the programs they are interested in applying to.

The name WriteFit stems from my belief that the right fit is an important part of the college process from what I have seen in students over the years working in education and being on admissions committees. What’s right for one student may not be right for another, and the process of figuring that out is as important in helping a student thrive as any external outcomes.

Applying for colleges can be stressful, but it is also a great opportunity for students to reflect on their goals and where they want to go in the future. I try to make the experience an enjoyable one for students so that they can learn more about themselves in the process and hone the writing and self-reflection skills that will not only strengthen their college applications, but will also serve them even afterwards.
