Hi! I’m Melissa.

I know the college application process can be a stressful time for many students, and I’m here to help. As an MIT alum and former admissions officer, I’ve read more than 6000 domestic and international applications and participated in admissions committee discussions. I also worked closely with the 4000+ global alumni interviewer network at MIT, giving me a firsthand look at how essays, extracurriculars, the interview, academics, and personal context come together to shape an admissions reader's understanding of a student.

Today, I work with MIT undergraduates as an academic advisor and volunteer with Minds Matter Boston, where I mentor high-achieving students from low-income backgrounds through the college process. These experiences have shown me all sides of the application process, from the early high school years to the senior year application madness and even to college life after the application.

Many students find the college essay intimidating because they think they have to write what the admissions committee wants to hear, but what they are really looking for is who you are as a person and what you will contribute to campus. As an essay coach, my goal is to help you figure out what that story is and how best to express it in conjunction with the other parts of your application, but also to make sure that each student leaves feeling like they’ve gained more clarity on who they are and where they’re going. College is an important step, but the skills you gain and the reflection you do for the application help prepare you not only for a higher education program but also for life beyond.
